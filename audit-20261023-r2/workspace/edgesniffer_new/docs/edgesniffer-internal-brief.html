Project overview

What we are building

EdgeSniffer is an on-chain radar. We study how active wallets trade, identify repeated clues around their decisions, and build an independent detector for those conditions.

Simple version: watch what a skilled wallet does, understand what was happening around the trade, then check whether the same opportunity is still available when an independent system sees it.

Study behavior

Use historical windows and live tracking to see what appears before and after a wallet decision.

Find fingerprints

Separate the clues: venue, route, timing, market cap, flow, liquidity, and pressure.

Test independently

Do not assume copying works. Model the price, delay, size, fees, and exit we could actually get.

Current evidence

We have found repeatable clues. Live profitability is not claimed yet.

The reverse-engineering work has identified repeated 2CQ conditions and route fingerprints. Historical simulations have produced positive paper results. The next step is proving whether those results survive independent timing and realistic execution.

Workflow

Five steps from a wallet transaction to a tested opportunity.

01 - WATCHCollect the tape

Watch the blockchain quickly and continuously.

ShredStream, gRPC, finalized transactions, native receive timestamps.

02 - LEARNFind the clues

Measure what tends to appear before the wallet acts.

Route, venue, timing, market cap, flow, liquidity, pressure.

03 - UNDERSTANDRebuild the market

Calculate the real price and available liquidity.

Venue-native curve or pool state. Unknown routes fail closed.

04 - SIMULATETest the trade

Try realistic sizes and selling rules.

One-second delay, fees, impact, slippage, failed trades, exits.

05 - PROVEUse fresh data

Test the frozen idea on events it has never seen.

Out-of-sample prospective validation before execution.

Simple explanation: learn from the past, watch the present, calculate what we could really have traded, and only trust a candidate that works on new events.

Presenter FAQ

Questions that may come up.

Start with the first sentence. Use the technical detail only if needed.

What is EdgeSniffer?

It is an on-chain radar that learns repeated wallet behavior and tests whether the same setup is still tradable for an independent system.

Are we live trading?

No. The current system is receive-only and research-only. No live trading result is being claimed.

What have we actually proven?

We have reconstructed observable 2CQ behavior and identified repeated route, timing, flow, and market-state clues. Positive historical simulations support the hypothesis. Fresh live-parity profitability is still the next proof gate.

Why not simply copy 2CQ?

Because the wallet may get a better price before an independent system can react. The important question is which buys still look favorable one second later.

What does fingerprint mean?

A fingerprint is a repeated combination of clues: where the trade happens, when it happens, what the market looks like, and what the surrounding flow looks like.

Why are the positive PnLs only simulations?

Because the system has not placed those trades with real money. Simulation is useful evidence, but it can be optimistic if timing or fills are unrealistic. The corrected work includes delay, fees, slippage, impact, and failed trades.

Why does the one-second delay matter?

It represents the time between seeing another wallet’s action and being able to act independently. A signal that only works at the wallet’s exact fill price is not enough.

Why are there 13 arms but 12 fingerprints?

There are 12 named venue and route lanes. The thirteenth is an ambiguous-data safety bucket. It is not a strategy and cannot produce economics until the venue is proven.

Can it handle 0.5 or 1 SOL?

That is why we test 0.1, 0.5, and 1.0 SOL separately. A strategy that only works at tiny size is not enough.

What happens if a route is unknown?

We keep the raw evidence for later decoder work, but fail closed and exclude it from PnL conclusions. Guessing would create false confidence.

How does this scale?

The stream, lineage, filtering, and research engine are reusable. Each venue gets its own adapter, so adding coverage does not require pretending different markets work the same way.

How close are we to live trading?

The infrastructure is moving toward native timing telemetry, quote/live-parity paper evidence, and small controlled execution calibration. That is progress, not a promised launch date or profitability claim.

What would make us reject a strategy?

If it fails to beat controls after realistic costs on fresh data, that route is rejected or returned to research. We do not hide a losing lane by blending it with another venue.
