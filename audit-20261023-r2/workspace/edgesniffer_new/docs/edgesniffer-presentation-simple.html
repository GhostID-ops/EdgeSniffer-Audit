EdgeSniffer studies how successful wallets trade, finds the repeated clues around those decisions, and turns those clues into an independent market sniffer.

In plain English: it is a fast on-chain radar. It watches for the same kind of setup that appears before a skilled wallet buys, then asks whether the opportunity is still there when we arrive.
Research infrastructure online - receive-only - trading disabled
The vision

Learn from the best traders, then become independent from them.

The goal is not to copy one wallet forever. The goal is to reverse-engineer repeatable behavior, detect the same conditions across the market, and eventually size and sell intelligently.

The long-term product: a scalable sniffer that can study a wallet, identify its fingerprints, watch many venues at once, and surface only opportunities that still make economic sense after delay, liquidity, fees, and price impact.

1. Study behavior

Use historical windows and live tracking to understand what happened before a wallet acted.

2. Find fingerprints

Separate the clues: venue, route, timing, market cap, flow, liquidity, and pressure.

3. Test independently

Do not assume copying works. Test what an independent system could actually buy and sell.

How the sniffer works

Five steps from a wallet transaction to a tested opportunity.

01 - WATCHCollect the tape

Watch the blockchain quickly and continuously.

ShredStream, gRPC, finalized transactions, native receive timestamps.

02 - LEARNFind the clues

Measure what tends to appear before the wallet acts.

Route, venue, timing, market cap, flow, liquidity, pressure.

03 - UNDERSTANDRebuild the market

Calculate the real price and available liquidity.

Venue-native curve or pool state. Unknown routes fail closed.

04 - SIMULATETest the trade

Try realistic sizes and selling rules.

One-second delay, fees, impact, slippage, failed trades, exits.

05 - PROVEUse fresh data

Test the frozen idea on events it has never seen.

Out-of-sample prospective validation before execution.

Questions you may get

The short, honest answers.

Lead with the plain-English answer. Use the technical detail only if they want to go deeper.

What is EdgeSniffer in one sentence?

It is an on-chain radar that learns repeatable wallet behavior and tests whether the same setup is still tradable for an independent system.

Are we live trading?

No. The current system is receive-only and research-only. No live trading result is being claimed.

What have we actually proven?

We have successfully reconstructed observable 2CQ behavior and identified repeated route, timing, flow, and market-state clues. Positive historical simulations support the hypothesis. Fresh live-parity profitability is still the next proof gate.

Why not simply copy 2CQ?

Because the wallet often gets a better price before an independent system can react. The important question is which 2CQ buys still look favorable one second later.

What does “fingerprint” mean?

A fingerprint is a repeated combination of clues: where the trade happens, when it happens, what the market looks like, and what the surrounding buying pressure looks like.

What does the graph mean?

The bright line is incoming blockchain data. The dotted line is the smaller subset with enough verified information to run a fair simulation. It measures data quality and coverage, not PnL.

Why is the one-second delay important?

It represents the time between seeing another wallet’s action and being able to act independently. A signal that only works at the wallet’s exact fill price is not enough.

Why are the positive PnLs only simulation?

Because the system has not placed those trades with real money. Simulation is useful evidence, but it can be too optimistic if timing or fills are unrealistic, so the corrected work includes delay, fees, slippage, impact, and failed trades.

Why are there 13 arms if there are 12 fingerprints?

There are 12 named venue/route lanes. The thirteenth is an ambiguous-data safety bucket. It is not a strategy and cannot produce economics until the venue is proven.

Can it handle 0.5 or 1 SOL?

That is why we test 0.1, 0.5, and 1.0 SOL separately. A strategy that only works at tiny size is not enough.

What happens if a route is unknown?

We keep the raw evidence for later decoder work, but fail closed and exclude it from PnL conclusions. Guessing would create fake confidence.

How does this scale?

The stream, lineage, filtering, and research engine are reusable. Each venue gets its own exact adapter, so adding coverage does not require pretending different markets work the same way.

How close are we to live trading?

The infrastructure is converging on the remaining gates: native timing telemetry, quote/live-parity paper evidence, and small controlled execution calibration. That is encouraging progress, not a guarantee of a launch date or profitability.

What would make us reject a strategy?

If it fails to beat controls after realistic costs on fresh data, that route is rejected or returned to research. We do not hide a losing arm by blending it with another venue.
