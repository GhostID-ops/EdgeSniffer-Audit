Sniffer Presentation

A guided explanation of what EdgeSniffer is, how we study wallet behavior, what the simulations have shown, and what still needs to be proved.

One-sentence version: EdgeSniffer studies the conditions around successful wallet trades and builds an independent detector for similar opportunities, while checking whether they are still tradable after delay and real costs.
Research-only system - receive-only collection - trading disabled
Project overview

What we are building

We are not trying to copy one wallet forever. We are using 2CQ's activity as a source of labeled examples, learning the conditions that repeatedly appeared around its decisions, and turning those conditions into a market-wide sniffer.

In normal language: we study what was happening before a skilled trader bought, look for the same setup elsewhere, and then ask whether an independent system could still enter and exit profitably.

Learn from behavior

Use many past decisions to separate repeatable conditions from one-off lucky trades.

Detect the setup

Look for the combination of flow, volume, liquidity, price movement, market stage, timing, and route.

Test independently

Do not assume the wallet's fill is our fill. Model our delay, size, fees, price impact, and exit.

Evidence so far

We have found repeatable clues. Live profitability is still unproven.

The research has successfully reconstructed observable 2CQ behavior and produced positive historical paper simulations. The important remaining question is whether those clues survive independent arrival time, realistic execution, and fresh data.

What we know and what remains

Separate the achievement from the remaining proof.

What we have established

  • 2CQ's observable trading behavior can be reconstructed.
  • Repeated route, timing, flow, liquidity, and market-stage clues exist.
  • Historical simulations can produce positive paper results.
  • Venue-specific decoding and cost-aware research infrastructure exist.
  • The system can preserve receive-only evidence without enabling trades.

What is not established yet

  • A strategy that is consistently profitable with live-equivalent timing.
  • That historical PnL survives current market conditions.
  • That a 0.5 or 1 SOL order can enter and exit without unacceptable impact.
  • That one exit policy works across all venues and regimes.
  • That any candidate should be promoted to execution.
Historical window method

We reconstruct the moment around each wallet decision.

A historical window is not just “the token went up after the wallet bought.” It is an ordered replay of what was visible before the decision, what the wallet actually did, and what happened afterward.

Before the buy

Pre-entry state

What did an independent system know before acting?

  • Was this token already appearing in similar activity?
  • Did volume increase over the last 5, 10, or 20 seconds?
  • Were buys increasing faster than sells?
  • Was liquidity enough for 0.1, 0.5, or 1 SOL?
  • Was the token already too extended?
  • What were market cap, reserves, price, and recent flow?
The decision

Verify what happened

Was this a genuine trade, and what exactly did it use?

  • Was it a buy rather than a transfer or setup event?
  • Which venue, pool, curve, and route were involved?
  • What were the price, size, fees, and timestamp?
  • Which accounts prove the token movement?
  • Was the raw evidence complete and correctly decoded?
After the buy

Measure the opportunity

Could an independent trader actually have benefited?

  • Did price continue after 1, 5, or 10 seconds?
  • Did other wallets follow the move?
  • How quickly did buying pressure reverse?
  • Could a realistic exit reach its target or stop?
  • How much favorable movement and drawdown occurred?
Why this matters: the wallet's profitable fill is not automatically our profitable fill. We use only information available before our entry, apply the one-second boundary, and measure exits only after entry.
Working clues from simulations

These are the fingerprints we actually mean.

Venue names such as Stonk.fun or PumpSwap describe where a trade happened. They are not, by themselves, reasons to buy. The useful fingerprints are repeated market conditions that appeared around stronger simulated opportunities.

Status: discovery clues only. They are not frozen live rules and still require fresh native-timing validation.
Clue 01 - strongest family

Buying pressure survives the delay

After waiting one second, recent buys still outweigh sells instead of the move already fading.

What we measure

Net flow during the delay, whether it stays above zero, and whether the result survives at each research size.

Positive-flow plus 5% activation / 3% trailing over 60 seconds was positive across the tested sizes: +1.485 SOL at 0.1 SOL, +5.952 SOL at 0.5 SOL, and +7.945 SOL at 1 SOL. Timing ambiguity remained.

Clue 02 - stricter family

Buying pressure and price agree

Buyers are still active and price has not deteriorated during the one-second wait.

What we measure

Positive net flow plus price continuation between the original observation and the independent entry boundary.

This was also positive in discovery, but it produced fewer eligible trades. That quality-versus-volume tradeoff needs prospective testing.

Clue 03 - room to trade

Liquidity can absorb our order

There is enough available liquidity that a 0.5 or 1 SOL order does not move the price too severely.

What we measure

Available quote liquidity, price impact, token output, and whether a later sell can be completed.

A 50 SOL liquidity filter was tested in the PumpSwap study. It is a research filter, not a proven universal cutoff.

Clue 04 - cost control

Fees do not consume the edge

A trade must remain attractive after pool, creator, protocol, and execution costs.

What we measure

Entry fees, exit fees, price impact, stress slippage, and final net PnL.

An entry-fee threshold of 120 basis points was tested as a filter. It reduced the sample and remains a hypothesis.

Clue 05 - 2CQ paper lane

Earlier market stage can matter

The 2CQ paper lane improved when the market-cap entry range was tightened.

What we measure

Market cap, recent flow, liquidity, and whether the signal survives the one-second delay.

The sub-10k cut improved PF in that dataset, but it is not automatically transferable to every venue or period.

Clue 06 - timing

The move is early, not exhausted

The setup appears before the main move is finished rather than after a large spike has already happened.

What we measure

Short-window volume acceleration, recent price change, buy/sell imbalance, and how far price already moved.

The five-second impulse family is a timing hypothesis that must be separated from late chasing.

What is not a fingerprint: Stonk.fun, PumpSwap, Raydium, Orca, and Meteora are venue or route categories. They tell us which market mechanics and decoder to use. The fingerprint is the combination of timing, flow, liquidity, price behavior, cost, and market stage.
Research workflow

How an observation becomes a research result.

Each stage answers a simple question before the next stage can use the data.

01 - SEE ITCapture activity

What is happening on-chain?

Transactions, account changes, arrival time, and raw instruction data.

02 - UNDERSTAND ITIdentify the setup

What conditions came before the wallet acted?

Flow, volume, liquidity, market cap, route, and timing.

03 - VERIFY ITConfirm the market

Do we know exactly where the trade happened?

Correct curve or pool, fees, reserves, and token movement.

04 - TRY IT ON PAPERModel our trade

Could we enter and exit after the signal?

Delay, size, impact, slippage, fees, failures, and exits.

05 - CHECK NEW DATAValidate it

Does the idea work on events it never saw?

Fresh prospective evidence before promotion.

Simple version: see it, understand it, verify it, simulate it, then test it on new data. If any stage is uncertain, the result stays research-only.
Simulation rules

What selling rules are being compared?

There is no single proven SL/TP formula yet. We compare exit families because different opportunities may be quick scalps, protected runners, or untradeable after the delay.

Fixed-time exits

Close after 5, 10, 15, 30, 60, 120, or 300 seconds.

This measures how the opportunity changes with holding time.

Take-profit / stop-loss

Test 5% take-profit with 5% stop-loss, alongside other pre-registered threshold variants.

The level must be reached by the post-entry path; missing marks are not invented.

Protection and partial exits

Test trailing protection, profit protection, and taking 50% off at a defined profit level.

These are hypotheses. No rule is promoted from the same data used to discover it.

Important: the 30-second PumpSwap rule is a frozen research challenge, not a universal strategy. The independent lab compares the full exit surface at 0.1, 0.5, and 1.0 SOL.
Venue coverage, not fingerprints

Where the clues can appear.

These are implementation buckets. They tell us which decoder and economics model to use; they do not automatically tell us a trade is good.

Pump.fun

SOL curve and CPI routes

Early token markets using Pump.fun's launch mechanics, either directly or through an inner program call.

PumpSwap

Direct and CPI routes

Trades against PumpSwap pools, with direct and routed paths kept separate.

Launch systems

Raydium and Stonk.fun

Early launch markets requiring their own lifecycle and fee interpretation.

Resolved pools

Raydium CLMM, Orca, Meteora

Router trades where the actual destination pool must be identified before calculating price impact.

Other protocols

Native protocol-specific routes

Unknown exchanges are split by exact program instead of being pooled into one generic result.

Safety bucket

Ambiguous evidence

Raw data is retained, but no economics are admitted without a proven venue.

Glossary

Plain-English translations.

Wallet

An on-chain account whose activity we study.

Fingerprint

A repeated combination of market clues that appears before a decision.

Venue

The market or exchange where the trade actually happens.

Route

The path a transaction takes to complete the trade.

Bonding curve

A built-in launch market whose price changes according to a formula as people buy and sell.

CPI

One on-chain program asking another program to perform part of the same transaction.

Liquidity

How much value is available for buying or selling without moving the price too much.

Slippage

The difference between the expected price and the price actually available.

Price impact

The price movement caused by our own order.

Paper PnL

The simulated profit or loss from replaying historical data.

Profit factor

Total simulated winning profit divided by total simulated losing loss.

Control group

A comparable set of opportunities used to check whether a filter adds value.

Out-of-sample

New data that was not used to choose the rule.

Receive-only

The system observes and records; it does not submit trades.

Native timing

The timestamp from when our own collector received the event, not a later reconstructed timestamp.

Risks and failure modes

What can make a promising result fail?

Timing

The wallet may get a better price before our signal arrives. A one-second delay can change the trade completely.

Liquidity

A strategy that works at 0.1 SOL may move the market or fail to exit at 0.5 or 1 SOL.

Market regime

Momentum, liquidity, and trader behavior can change across different periods.

Data quality

Unknown routes, missing marks, failed transactions, and incomplete lineage must not become invented prices.

Overfitting

Choosing the best filter and exit from the same sample can create a result that does not repeat.

Wallet drift

The wallet may change behavior, use new venues, or stop trading the way it did historically.

Presenter FAQ

Questions that may come up.

Use the first sentence as the short answer and expand only if needed.

What is EdgeSniffer?

It is an on-chain radar that learns repeated wallet behavior and tests whether the same setup is still tradable for an independent system.

What have we actually proven?

We have reconstructed observable 2CQ behavior and identified repeated market clues. Positive historical simulations support the hypothesis. Fresh live-parity profitability is still unproven.

Are we live trading?

No. The current system is receive-only and research-only.

Why not simply copy 2CQ?

Because the wallet may get a better price before an independent system can react. The key question is which buys still look favorable after our delay.

Are the fingerprints venues?

No. Venues tell us where to decode the trade. Fingerprints are the repeated conditions: flow, volume, liquidity, price behavior, costs, timing, and market stage.

Why are the positive PnLs simulations?

Because the system has not placed those trades with real money. Simulation is useful evidence, but it can be optimistic if timing or fills are unrealistic.

What does CPI mean?

It means one on-chain program calls another program inside the same transaction. We follow that handoff to identify what was really bought and where.

Can it handle 0.5 or 1 SOL?

That is why every serious candidate is tested at 0.1, 0.5, and 1 SOL with liquidity and price impact included.

What is the SL/TP strategy?

There is no single proven one. We compare fixed-time exits, 5% take-profit / 5% stop-loss, trailing protection, profit protection, and partial exits as research hypotheses.

What happens when a route is unknown?

We keep the evidence for decoder work but fail closed and exclude it from PnL conclusions.

How do you prevent overfitting?

Candidate rules are selected from discovery data, then frozen and tested on a separate fresh cohort. A positive result from the discovery sample alone is not validation.

How close are we to live trading?

The infrastructure is moving toward native timing telemetry, quote/live-parity paper evidence, and small controlled execution calibration. That is progress, not a promise of profitability or a launch date.

What would make us reject a strategy?

If it fails to beat controls after realistic costs on fresh data, that route is rejected or returned to research.
