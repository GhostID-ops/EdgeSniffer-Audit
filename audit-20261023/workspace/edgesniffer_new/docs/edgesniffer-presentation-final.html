Sniffer Presentation

A simple walkthrough of what EdgeSniffer is, how we study wallet behavior, what we have learned from 2CQ, and what still needs to be proven.

One-sentence explanation: EdgeSniffer watches the blockchain for the same kinds of conditions that appear before a skilled wallet trades, then checks whether an independent system could still act on those conditions.
Research-only system - receive-only collection - trading disabled
01 / Project overview

What we are building

2CQ is the wallet we are studying. We examine its historical trades and live activity to discover repeated conditions around its decisions. EdgeSniffer is the independent detector we are building from those clues.

Simple version: study what happened before a skilled wallet bought, identify the clues that repeat, and see whether those clues still appear when we arrive independently.

Study the behavior

Rebuild the market around many past decisions instead of looking at one winning token.

Find the fingerprints

Separate the clues: recent buying, volume, token age, market cap, liquidity, venue, route, and timing.

Test it independently

Do not assume the wallet's price is our price. Model delay, fees, size, impact, and exits.

03 / Historical windows

We reconstruct the moment around each wallet decision.

A historical window is a short, ordered replay of the market around one decision. We use it to discover what information existed before the wallet acted and what happened after.

Before the buy

Pre-entry state

What could an independent system have seen?

  • Did similar tokens show activity?
  • Did volume rise over 5, 10, or 20 seconds?
  • Was buying pressure accelerating or fading?
  • Was liquidity enough for 0.1, 0.5, or 1 SOL?
  • Was the token already too extended?
The wallet action

Decision and proof

What exactly happened on-chain?

  • Was it a genuine buy rather than a transfer or setup event?
  • Which market and route were used?
  • What were price, size, fees, reserves, and market cap?
  • Was another program involved inside the transaction?
After the buy

Outcome window

What would have happened after our arrival?

  • Did price continue after 1, 5, or 10 seconds?
  • Did other traders follow?
  • How quickly did pressure reverse?
  • Could a realistic exit reach its target or stop?
Why this matters: the wallet's profitable fill is not automatically our profitable fill. The window lets us separate information available before the decision from information that only became visible afterward.
04 / Workflow

How an observation becomes a research result.

Every stage answers one simple question before the next stage is allowed to use the data.

01 - SEE ITCapture activity

What is happening on-chain right now?

Record transactions, account changes, arrival time, and raw instruction data.

02 - UNDERSTAND ITIdentify the setup

What conditions appeared before the wallet acted?

Compare recent flow, volume, liquidity, market cap, route, and timing.

03 - VERIFY ITConfirm the market

Do we know exactly where the trade happened?

Decode the correct curve or pool, fees, reserves, and token movement.

04 - TRY IT ON PAPERModel our trade

Could we have entered and exited after the signal?

Apply delay, size, impact, slippage, fees, failed trades, and exit rules.

05 - CHECK NEW DATAValidate the idea

Does it work on events not used to create it?

Require a fresh prospective sample before promotion.

Simple version: see it, understand it, verify it, simulate it, then test it on new data. If a stage is uncertain, the result stays research-only.
05 / Simulation rules

How entries and exits are tested.

There is not one universal SL/TP setting. We compare several pre-registered exit families to learn whether a setup behaves like a quick scalp, a protected runner, or no executable opportunity.

Fixed-time exits

Close after 5, 10, 15, 30, 60, 120, or 300 seconds.

This shows how results change with holding time.

Take-profit and stop-loss

Test a 5% take-profit with a 5% stop-loss, using 15, 30, 60, and 120-second maximum windows.

The level must actually be reached after entry; it is not assumed from a later high or low.

Protection and partial selling

Also test profit protection, trailing exits, and taking 50% off at +5% while managing the remainder.

These are research hypotheses, not a frozen live policy.

Important: the historical 2CQ lanes contain separate runner and scalper profiles, while the independent lab compares the broader exit surface. No single SL/TP formula has been declared universally proven.

08 / Plain-English glossary

Terms that may come up.

PnL
Profit and loss: how much the modeled trade gained or lost after its assumptions.
PF
Profit factor: total modeled winning profit divided by total modeled losing loss. Above 1 means wins were larger in total than losses.
Liquidity
How much buying and selling the market can absorb without moving the price too much.
Slippage
The difference between the price we expect and the price we actually receive.
Price impact
How much our own order moves the price against us.
TP / SL
Take-profit and stop-loss: planned levels for closing a position in profit or limiting a loss.
Venue
The actual market where the trade happened, such as Pump.fun, PumpSwap, Raydium, Orca, or Meteora.
Route
The path the transaction took to reach that market.
Pool / curve
The on-chain pricing system that determines how much a buy or sell receives.
CPI
A transaction inside a transaction: one Solana program asks another program to complete part of the action.
Control
A comparable token or trade used to check whether the selected setup actually outperformed ordinary alternatives.
Prospective test
A test on new events collected after the rule was fixed, so the rule cannot be tuned to its own answers.
09 / Presenter FAQ

Questions that may come up.

Start with the first sentence. Use the detail only if someone wants to go deeper.

What is EdgeSniffer?

It is an on-chain radar that learns repeated wallet behavior and tests whether the same setup is still tradable for an independent system.

Who is 2CQ?

2CQ is the wallet we are reverse-engineering. Its activity gives us examples to study; the finished sniffer is meant to detect conditions independently, not blindly copy the wallet forever.

What have we actually proven?

We have reconstructed observable 2CQ behavior and identified repeated route, timing, flow, and market-state clues. Positive historical simulations support the hypothesis. Fresh live-parity profitability is still the next proof gate.

Are we live trading?

No. The current system is receive-only and research-only. No live trading result is being claimed.

Was any real transaction attempted?

One isolated 0.001 SOL executor-calibration attempt was authorized to test submission and inclusion telemetry. It was not a strategy trade: the chain did not confirm inclusion, no position was proven, and no exit was sent.

Why are the positive PnLs only simulations?

Because the system has not placed those trades with real money. Simulation is useful evidence, but it can be optimistic if timing or fills are unrealistic.

Why not simply copy 2CQ?

The wallet may get a better price before an independent system can react. The important question is which buys still look favorable one second later.

Why are there 13 arms but 12 fingerprints?

There are 12 named market and route lanes. The thirteenth is an ambiguous-data safety bucket. It is not a strategy and cannot produce economics until the market is proven.

Can it handle 0.5 or 1 SOL?

That is why we test 0.1, 0.5, and 1.0 SOL separately. A strategy that only works at tiny size is not enough.

What happens if a route is unknown?

We keep the raw evidence for later decoder work, but fail closed and exclude it from PnL conclusions. Guessing would create false confidence.

How close are we to live trading?

The infrastructure is moving toward native timing telemetry, quote/live-parity paper evidence, and small controlled execution calibration. That is progress, not a promised launch date or profitability claim.

What would make us reject a strategy?

If it fails to beat controls after realistic costs on fresh data, that route is rejected or returned to research. We do not hide a losing lane by blending it with another market.

What is the biggest risk?

Market conditions can change, liquidity can disappear, transactions can fail, and historical patterns can stop working. That is why the final gates require fresh data and realistic execution assumptions.

03 / The actual 2CQ fingerprint

2CQ is not simply buying early. It is detecting an early burst of coordinated demand.

The strongest preliminary SOL-Pump choice analysis found a very specific pattern around the wallet's selected entries. These numbers describe what 2CQ chose, not guaranteed profit from copying it.

How early
67 ms

Very young tokens

Selected tokens had a median age of about 67 milliseconds from creation, compared with roughly 49 seconds for comparable controls.

Plain English: 2CQ is looking almost immediately after a token appears.

How recently
46 ms

Fresh activity

The latest outside trade before the selected decision was a median 46 milliseconds old, versus about 20 seconds for controls.

Plain English: 2CQ is reacting to activity that just happened, not stale activity.

3 buys / 4.17 SOL

Fast buy impulse

Selected entries showed a median three buys and 4.17 SOL of gross buying in the first 250 milliseconds.

Plain English: several meaningful buys arrive almost together.

First second
4.94 gross / 4.00 net

Demand holds immediately

Within the first second, selected entries had about 4.94 SOL gross buying and 4.00 SOL net buying at the median.

Plain English: most of the early money is buying rather than selling.

Participants
3 buyers

More than one wallet

The selected entries had a median of three unique buyers, with a mean buy around 1.73 SOL and the largest buy around 2.02 SOL.

Plain English: the signal is a cluster of serious participants, not only one isolated wallet.

Decision boundary

What the sniffer must do

Detect the burst quickly, verify the exact market, wait through the independent one-second boundary, and reject cases where the demand has already weakened.

This is the bridge from reverse-engineering to an independent strategy.

Important distinction: the 67ms, 46ms, 250ms, and 1-second figures describe the teacher-wallet fingerprint. The current executable paper profile adds its own guards, including low market cap and net-buy flow. The teacher fingerprint is the clue; the executable profile is the testable implementation.

What is already supported

The preliminary SOL-Pump choice model separated 2CQ's selected entries from controls with 88.62% top-1 accuracy on 123 fair decisions. This is evidence that the wallet's choice is structurally identifiable.

It is not alpha proof and it did not read PnL outcomes.

What is still open

Whether an independent system can detect the same burst early enough, enter after the real delay, survive fees and impact, and produce positive out-of-sample economics.

That is why the independent strategy lab and fresh prospective cohort remain necessary.

10 / How to read the numbers

What the fingerprint measurements mean

These are summaries across many historical 2CQ decisions, not claims that every token looked exactly the same.

“Median 3 unique buyers”

Median means the middle result after all observations are sorted. If the median is 3, about half the selected entries had 3 or fewer different buyers and about half had 3 or more. It does not mean every entry had exactly 3.

Unique buyers means different wallet addresses. If wallets A, B, and C each buy, that is 3 unique buyers. If wallet A buys three times, that is still only 1 unique buyer.

Example: buyer counts of 1, 2, 3, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 8 have a middle value of 3. The metric describes the typical centre of the sample, not a promise about the next token.

“67 milliseconds” and “250 milliseconds”

1,000 milliseconds equals 1 second. So 67ms is about 0.067 seconds. A 250ms window is one quarter of a second.

Those short windows help us ask whether buying arrived as a rapid burst immediately after token creation, rather than slowly accumulating over minutes.

Example: three buys spread across 10 minutes are not the same clue as three buys arriving inside 250ms.

Gross flow versus net flow

Gross buying adds every buy. Net buying subtracts selling from that activity. Net flow is closer to the question “is demand still winning after sellers are included?”

Example: 5 SOL of buys and 1 SOL of sells means 5 SOL gross buying, but 4 SOL net buying.

Selected entries versus controls

A selected entry is a token 2CQ actually chose. A control is a comparable token from the same research universe that was not chosen. Comparing them tests whether a clue is distinctive or merely common market activity.

For example, 67ms median token age for selected entries versus roughly 49 seconds for controls suggests 2CQ was choosing unusually fresh tokens in this historical sample.

What 88.62% does—and does not—mean

The preliminary choice model correctly separated the selected 2CQ entry from comparable alternatives in 88.62% of 123 fair historical decisions.

That means the wallet’s choice pattern was recognizable in that sample. It does not mean 88.62% of trades were profitable, and it does not prove a live strategy.

Why these numbers matter

The measurements turn “2CQ buys good tokens” into testable questions: how young is the token, how recent is the activity, how fast do other wallets arrive, is net demand still positive, and can an independent system act before the burst disappears?

The next step is to test those rules with real delay, fees, liquidity, failed trades, and fresh data.

11 / The actual finish line

Find an independent rule that still works after 2CQ is gone.

The goal is not to prove that 2CQ made good trades. The goal is to use 2CQ's history to identify conditions that an independent sniffer can recognize, enter after its real delay, and trade at realistic size.

What we already have

Historical wallet activity, route and timing clues, a recognizable early-demand fingerprint, venue-specific decoders, and paper research infrastructure.

What must still be proven

After the one-second boundary, the setup must survive real liquidity, fees, slippage, price impact, failed trades, and an executable exit.

What counts as success

A pre-registered rule beats contemporaneous controls on fresh prospective data at 0.1, 0.5, and 1.0 SOL, with realistic drawdown and no hidden look-ahead.

Simple version: 2CQ gives us the clues. The sniffer must independently find the remaining opportunity. Only fresh, realistic, out-of-sample results can promote a strategy.
